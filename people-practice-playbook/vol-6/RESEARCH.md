## Workplace evidence: pregnancy loss

- **Kelly-Harrington et al. (2025).** Workplace supports for early pregnancy loss: a scoping review of international literature. *Work* 81(1), 2021–2047. doi:10.1177/10519815241305007.
  - Read: full text, via a free copy hosted by Star Legacy Foundation. Licence not stated.
  - Covers loss before viability (miscarriage, ectopic, molar, termination), 2012 to 2022. 48 records, of which 15 are journal articles.
  - Finds little empirical research. Leave is the most discussed support. Many people used sick, annual or unpaid leave.
  - In one UK survey, 24% told neither their manager nor HR. People with losses before 20 weeks were least likely to tell.
  - Men reported less support. Gaps in policies covering surrogacy. Pressure to "be professional".
  - Every empirical study was from a high-income, English-speaking country.
- **Donaghy, Lewis, Keep and Carland (2026).** "I was told I either came back or I lost my job". *Community, Work & Family* 29(1), 86–100. doi:10.1080/13668803.2024.2350550.
  - Read: full text. Licence CC BY-NC-ND 4.0.
  - 13 Australian women, early pregnancy loss.
  - Themes: pressure to return too soon (open, subtle and from within); decisions about telling people (the "12-week rule", fear of being seen as less capable, privacy); flexibility. They valued having control, because they couldn't predict how they'd feel.
  - None used paid bereavement leave, and some didn't know it existed.
- **Obst, Due, Oxlad and Middleton (2022).** Australian men's experiences of leave provisions and workplace support following pregnancy loss or neonatal death. *Community, Work & Family* 25(4), 551–562. doi:10.1080/13668803.2020.1823319.
  - Read: accepted manuscript, free at the University of Adelaide.
  - 220 men. 91% told their workplace; 74% were offered leave.
  - Themes: emotional toll of returning; needing to be with and support their partner; recognition; work as a helpful distraction (some didn't want all the leave offered); pressure to return.
  - Convenience sample through support organisations. Losses up to 20 years earlier.
- **Rose and Oxlad (2023).** LGBTQ+ peoples' experiences of workplace leave and support following pregnancy loss. *Community, Work & Family* 26(2), 268–284. doi:10.1080/13668803.2021.2020727.
  - Read: abstract, plus details reported in the Darwin et al. synthesis.
  - 12 people, gestational and non-gestational parents.
  - Policies built around straight couples made leave harder to get and misgendered entitlements. They didn't allow for strong attachment early in a pregnancy, and made things hard for parents using surrogacy.
  - The authors recommend inclusive language, and leave regardless of how the family was formed or how far along the pregnancy was.
- **Darwin, Bainbridge, Bekiropoulou and Greenfield (2026).** Enduring hope and loss: qualitative evidence synthesis of LGBTQ+ experiences of perinatal loss. *Frontiers in Psychiatry* 16. doi:10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1732197.
  - Read: open-access full text (the summary covered about two-thirds of the page). Licence not yet confirmed.
  - 7 studies, about 145 people, all in the Global North. Only one study focused on trans men and non-binary people. No intersex or asexual participants.
  - Reports one person refused leave by HR on the grounds that neither partner had been pregnant.

## Workplace evidence: fertility treatment

- **Payne, Seenan and van den Akker (2019).** Experiences and psychological distress of fertility treatment and employment. *Journal of Psychosomatic Obstetrics & Gynecology*.
  - Read: accepted manuscript, Middlesex repository. DOI still to confirm.
  - 563 UK participants: 98% women, 95% white, recruited through Fertility Network UK.
  - 23% had a workplace policy for treatment. Having a policy and more support went with lower distress; telling someone alone didn't.
  - 72% told their employer. Average of 8.74 days off per cycle.
- **Wilkinson, Mumford and Carroll (2026).** Fertility and the workplace. *Gender, Work & Organization*. doi:10.1111/gwao.70136.
  - Read: full text. Licence CC BY.
  - 18 people working in UK higher education, from a wider study of 80 interviews.
  - People disguised treatment absences as sickness or generic appointments, and found that distressing. Informal arrangements were fragile and disappeared when their manager changed.
  - People put careers on hold or scaled them back. The authors recommend going beyond paid time off.

## Workplace evidence: telling people about a pregnancy

- **Verhoff and Buzzanell (2022).** "Well, I'm going to have a baby". *Communication Studies* 73(4), 425–440. doi:10.1080/10510974.2022.2097720.
  - Read: abstract only. Paywalled.
  - 92 women. Telling people at work is an ongoing process with many people involved. Themes include timing it safely, telling people selectively and controlling gatherings.

## Clinical bodies and definitions

- **World Health Organization, Infertility fact sheet** (28 Nov 2025). Read in full through the cloud browser.
  - Defines infertility as not getting pregnant after 12 months or more of regular unprotected sex.
  - About 1 in 6 people of reproductive age experience it in their lifetime.
  - Covers male, female and unexplained causes. Names same-sex partners, people not in sexual relationships and cancer survivors as people who may need fertility care.
  - Notes that women in a relationship with a man are often assumed to be the infertile partner, whether or not they are.
  - WHO issued its first global infertility guideline on 28 Nov 2025 (not opened).
- **Quenby et al. (2021).** Miscarriage matters. *The Lancet* 397(10285), 1658–1667. doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(21)00682-6.
  - Read: abstract. Free accepted manuscripts at Aalborg and Warwick (CC BY-NC-ND).
  - Pooled risk of miscarriage is 15.3% of recognised pregnancies. 10.8% of women have had one miscarriage. About 23 million miscarriages a year.
- **WHO and MedlinePlus fact sheets for miscarriage, ectopic, molar pregnancy and stillbirth: still to read.** WebFetch blocks WHO fact sheets and MedlinePlus in this session; the cloud browser can open them.

## Clinical descriptions found on 20 Nov 2026 (second pass)

- **Stillbirth.** WHO's health-topic page and UN figures use a cut-off of 28 weeks or more for international comparison.
  - WHO's Partnership for Maternal, Newborn and Child Health says WHO defines stillbirth from 22 weeks but recommends 28 weeks for comparing countries.
  - High-income countries often count stillbirth from 20 or 22 weeks; the US and Australia use 20 weeks.
  - So the line between miscarriage and stillbirth depends on the country, and so do the leave rights that follow from it. This is a legal point to flag, not write in.
- **Ectopic pregnancy.** MedlinePlus (current page): a pregnancy outside the womb, most often in a fallopian tube. It can't continue to birth and may be life threatening. Treatment means ending the pregnancy with medicine or surgery.
- **Molar pregnancy.** Only an archived MedlinePlus page seen so far. Still need the current page.

## Termination

- **Rocca et al. (2020).** Emotions and decision rightness over five years following an abortion. *Social Science & Medicine* 248, 112704. doi:10.1016/j.socscimed.2019.112704.
  - Read: abstract (Elsevier PDF in the US Congress record), plus the UCSF press release.
  - 667 US participants at 30 facilities, 2008 to 2010.
  - Predicted share feeling it was the right decision: 97.5% at the start, 99.0% at five years. Relief was the most common emotion.
  - Finding the decision hard, and sensing stigma in their community, predicted negative feelings later. That supports "feelings vary, and they aren't ours to judge".
  - The authors and UCSF frame it against US abortion laws. We use it only to show that relief is common.
  - More than 95% said it was the right decision at each point. Relief was the most common emotion. At five years, 84% had mainly positive feelings or none.

## Published policies

- **PayCaptain.**
  - Fertility Support policy, last reviewed 16 May 2024.
  - Pregnancy Loss and Bereavement policy, undated.
  - Open handbook with no licence stated. Read in full through the cloud browser on 20 Nov 2026.
- **Dublin City University.**
  - Pregnancy Loss and Fertility Leave Policy v1.0, approved 23 June 2026, plus a manager guidance page modified 11 Nov 2026. No licence stated. Read on 20 Nov 2026.
  - Covers all genders and partners. Includes same-sex couples, single people, surrogacy and adoption on the fertility side.
  - 10 paid days for loss before 24 weeks. 10 days per fertility cycle, capped at 30.
  - Lists abortion as a pregnancy loss in its definitions, but its leave section doesn't say whether abortion is covered.
  - Says a neutral, accepting or relieved response is as valid as grief.
  - Asks for a certificate from a GP or consultant.
  - Leave codes are visible only to the employee, their manager and the People team. Nothing is shared without written consent.
- **Channel 4.** Pregnancy Loss Policy, April 2021, published on its corporate site; press coverage says it was offered for other organisations to use. No licence stated. Full PDF read on 20 Nov 2026.
  - Covers people affected directly, through a partner or through a surrogate, at any length of service.
  - Lists abortion as a loss with no limit on the reason, plus miscarriage, stillbirth, ectopic, molar and neonatal loss. Embryo transfer isn't mentioned.
  - At least two weeks' paid leave, self-certified with no fit note. Paid time off for related appointments.
  - Recovery has no time limit, so further sickness leave after returning is allowed.
  - Makes no assumptions about how people feel. Offers a trained buddy and confidential contacts beyond the manager. Manager training is mandatory.
  - After a stillbirth it refers to "the female employee", tied to UK maternity law.
  - PayCaptain's loss policy looks close to it in places (camera-off calls, more breaks, the list of charities). Don't claim it was copied.
- **Other policies read on 20 Nov 2026, not chosen:**
  - University of Greenwich Pregnancy Loss Guidance, v1.0 dated 16 Mar 2026. Strong on inclusion: names abortion and TFMR separately, includes trans and non-binary people, and says people may prefer their own terms. But it calls itself a first draft, and it would make a second university.
  - University of Bath Reproductive Health Policy, approved 30 Apr 2026. Covers fertility, loss, menopause and menstruation together. It quotes uncited statistics, which could be traced for section 2.
- **Candidates for a third, before Channel 4:**
  - Good-Loop's Women's Health Policy: public summary only. It names termination alongside loss, but frames everything around being "biologically female".
  - Attest's fertility policy: a blog post.
  - The Miscarriage UK template (formerly the Miscarriage Association): the page has moved and the template now seems to come with its paid pledge.
  - The CharlieHR policy has been taken down.
