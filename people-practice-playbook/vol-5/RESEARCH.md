# People Practice Playbook, Vol. 5: Research notes

Working notes from the research behind Vol. 5, gathered 16 to 20 November 2026. Not reader-facing. The published text uses only the sources in vol5-SOURCES.md.

## Starting point

Three published policies, found through Open Org: Juro (Women's health), Human Made (Menopause Policy) and the Open Org Menopause Support Policy template. Read in full and compared side by side in section 6.

## Source rules for this volume

- Peer-reviewed research for any claim about what's happening or what works.
- Clinical bodies (WHO, MedlinePlus) for what a condition is. Never for advice.
- Government and standards bodies only to show what exists.
- Added after the audit: one large weighted survey report (Fawcett) and the three published policies, always labelled as such.
- Blogs, influencers and vendor material stay out.

## Left out on purpose

- **A US "1 in 10" menopause figure from a survey commissioned by Mira** (a hormone-monitoring brand, 1,000 women over 35). Vendor survey. Seen in search results only.
- **Juro's FemTech market-size figure** (an investor's blog post) and its audiobook link on cycle education. Section 6 describes the callout without linking it.
- **Treatments of any kind**, including hormone therapy, CBT and yoga. Rodrigo et al. found self-help CBT improved work outcomes; not used, because the series gives no health advice. Could be mentioned later as something some employers offer, with care.
- **Menstrual leave history** (Japan 1947, Indonesia 2003 and others). Seen in secondary sources; legal detail is out of scope.

## Couldn't be reached

- NHS pages (not on the allowlist). MedlinePlus used for PMS, PMDD and period pain instead.
- PMC and Taylor & Francis (bot checks). Free repository copies used instead; Rowson and Jones read from the Reading repository abstract.
- Juro and Open Org pages blocked the independent auditor. Both were read in full during drafting.

## Leads not yet opened

- Adegoke et al. (2025), Menstrual discrimination: period pain and productivity, *Equality, Diversity and Inclusion*. Found by the auditor via a university news page.
- An ISO standard on menopause at work, and a British Standard on menstruation and menopause at work. Headlines only.
- A 2016 professional guidance statement on menopause at work (Maturitas) mentioning cold drinking water and toilets. Search result only; the same points are sourced from Hardy 2017 instead.

## Decisions

- Scope: periods, perimenopause, menopause, and related conditions (endometriosis, PCOS, PMS and PMDD) for awareness only. Pregnancy, pregnancy loss, fertility and testosterone decline in cis men are left for their own volume.
- Inclusive by default: anyone who menstruates or goes through menopause, whatever their gender identity or sexuality. Support must never depend on disclosure.
- No ranking: the guide never treats one condition as more serious than another.
- Choices, not prescriptions: menstrual leave, attendance triggers, default versus on-request support and whether a reason is needed are all set out with trade-offs.
- Appointments: treated like any other medical appointment (Spec's take).
- Who someone talks to: the person's choice (Spec's take).
- Sample policies: named and credited, in alphabetical order, strengths first, never ranked.
- No cross-volume references: escalation routes are left for readers to look at internally rather than pointing to Vol. 2.
