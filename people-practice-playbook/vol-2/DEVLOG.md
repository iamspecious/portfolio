## Entry 001: People Practice Playbook, Vol. 2 v0.1, first full draft

**Date:** 9 November 2026
**Status:** Draft

### What and why

Volume 2 of the open People Practice Playbook series, on onboarding new hires from offer accepted to about six months in. Written for founders and executives, People and ops leads, line managers and the new hire themselves. Same research-led approach as Vol. 1: each section says what the research shows, good and bad, where it runs out, and then what I'd do.

### Key decisions

- Onboarding starts at offer accepted, but before day one is the organisation's work. The new hire gets one short message with the logistics, and no unpaid reading or tasks.
- Five pillars: Business, Operations, The role, People and culture, and Growth, with where to take a problem running underneath all five.
- Day counts and buddy length are each organisation's choice. There's no research on 30, 60 or 90 days, and the playbook says so.
- Explicitness runs through the whole thing rather than a separate chapter on adjustments, because being explicit helps everyone.
- Two fairness scenarios, written speaking-up norms that are never put down to nationality or background, and probation that doesn't read quiet as disengaged.
- Every example says "This is illustrative, not a real company."
- Templates in section 10, each usable on its own.

## Entry 002: People Practice Playbook, Vol. 2 v0.1.1, integrity audit fixes

**Date:** 9 November 2026
**Status:** Patch

### What and why

An integrity audit of v0.1, run in a fresh session against every source, passed it with fixes. No critical findings. This version applies all of them, and every changed claim was checked against its source again first.

### What changed

- Added a 2024 meta-analysis of 83 onboarding field experiments as the strongest evidence that onboarding changes whether people stay.
- Re-cited the Saks et al. (2007) meta-analysis to its own abstract, and corrected what the playbook says it found.
- Removed "short" from the realistic job preview finding. The research doesn't say previews should be short.
- Disclosed that the CIPD neuroinclusion report was co-written with the head of a training company.
- Reworded four unsourced "most onboarding" claims so none claims a consensus without a source.
- Replaced signs of "not feeling accepted" in section 3 that read quietness as a problem, which contradicted section 8.
- Fixed and relabelled links, corrected a publication year, softened several overstated phrases, added a caveat on camera norms, made the examples' pronouns consistent, and narrowed the buddy brief's confidentiality line.

### Key decisions

- Every fix was proposed before it went in, and checked against the source again first.
- One audit suggestion wasn't applied: I haven't read the Brandon Hall brief myself, so the wording about its figures stays as it is until I do.
