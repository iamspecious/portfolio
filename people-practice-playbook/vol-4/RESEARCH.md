# People Practice Playbook, Vol. 4: Research notes

Working notes from the research behind Vol. 4, gathered 12 November 2026. Not reader-facing. The published text uses only the sources in SOURCES.md.

## Starting point

Spec's earlier community post on safety plans: definition, who they're for, a six-part checklist, HR guidance, and a Canadian resource list. Every resource in it was re-checked against the service's own website.

## Corrections to the original resource list

- **Crisis Services Canada, 1-833-456-4566 / text 45645.** Outdated. The national line is 9-8-8 (call or text). crisisservicescanada.ca now redirects to talksuicide.ca. It Gets Better Canada's Get Help page still lists the old number. A search summary said the change happened on 30 November 2023; that date wasn't confirmed from a primary source, so the published text doesn't give it.
- **LGBT YouthLine, 1-800-268-9688.** YouthLine stopped phone calls to the HelpLine in 2023; text 647-694-4275 or chat. Ontario, 29 and under, peer support not crisis support. Its office number (on its Contact page) isn't the HelpLine. It's unconfirmed whether the 1-800 number is that office line, so the published text doesn't say.
- **Egale, "counselling and crisis services".** Egale's About page timeline describes Egale Youth OUTReach (2014) as counselling, homelessness and suicide crisis services for youth up to 29. The home page shows research, education, advocacy and a Find Support directory, with no current crisis line. Current status of Youth OUTReach not confirmed.
- **pflag Canada.** Peer support through chapters. Its contact page sends people in crisis to 9-8-8 or Kids Help Phone. It Gets Better Canada lists 1-888-530-6777 for pflag; that number isn't on pflag's own site, so it's left out.
- **Trans Lifeline.** Added. Its own page gives Mon to Fri, 1 to 9 pm Eastern; It Gets Better Canada's list gives 5 pm to 1 am Eastern. Used the service's own hours.
- **Kids Help Phone.** Confirmed. Serves young people.
- **DomesticShelters.org.** Confirmed it covers Canada.

## Researched but not used yet

- **Domestic violence safety planning trials.** Ford-Gilboe and colleagues (2020, BMC Public Health), iCAN Plan 4 Safety RCT with 462 Canadian women: search summary says both tailored and generic versions improved outcomes, with no difference between arms. Glass and colleagues (2017, Am J Prev Med), myPlan internet safety decision aid. Neither opened (Springer host not allowed). Candidate for section 3 in the next version.
- **Stanley and colleagues (2018, JAMA Psychiatry)**, safety planning with follow-up vs usual care. Included in Nuij's meta-analysis; not opened separately.
- **Labouliere, Stanley, Lake and Gould (2020)**, safety planning on crisis lines. Cited by 9-8-8 as the basis for its template; not opened.

## Leads from the integrity audit (not yet opened)

- **Children and adolescents:** a 2025 JAMA Pediatrics systematic review and meta-analysis of safety planning for young people (doi:10.1001/jamapediatrics.2025.1012) reportedly found no significant effect on suicide outcomes. Search results only; jamanetwork.com returned 403. Section 3 already says the Nuij results can't be assumed to hold for young people. Check before the next version.
- **Domestic violence leave:** the audit suspects "some offer protected leave" understates how many provinces and territories now have it. canada.ca didn't respond. Legal detail, so flag only.
- **Nunavut 9-1-1:** confirm current status from a Government of Nunavut source.

## Legal points flagged to Spec, kept out of the text

- Criminal Code s. 264 was amended in 2026 (c. 19, s. 27); the current text includes monitoring a person's location and says safety includes psychological safety. The playbook links the section and says nothing about what it covers.
- CCOHS notes Ontario's OHSA s. 32.0.4 names domestic violence, and Manitoba has Interpersonal Violence Leave. The playbook only says some jurisdictions name domestic violence and some offer protected leave, and points to CCOHS's legislation page.
- Duty to report: YouthLine describes Ontario's duty to report abuse of children under 16 to the Children's Aid Society. The playbook says only that reporting duties vary.
- 9-1-1 in Nunavut: Shelter Safe says it isn't available; not checked against a Nunavut government source. Attributed to Shelter Safe.
